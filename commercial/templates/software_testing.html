Concrete problems we solve

Start from the failure mode, not from a generic service catalogue.

Most teams do not need “more testing.” They need one expensive part of the verification flow to become faster, more deterministic or easier to maintain.

Regression

Runs take too long or need morning-after repair

Stabilize setup, timing, reusable helpers, verdict logic and machine-readable reporting so regression stops depending on tribal knowledge.

Diagnostics

UDS / DTC / OBD checks are still manual

Automate sessions, NRC handling, security flows, diagnostic parameters, DTC/OBD checks, wake-up, recovery and reset behaviour.

Fault handling

Negative paths are difficult to reproduce

Build deterministic fault injection around sensors, communication, supply boundaries and recovery criteria with explicit expected-versus-actual checks.

HIL throughput

The bench is scarce and too much work waits for it

Separate what truly needs HIL from what can move to SIL/PIL, then preserve reusable test intent across environments.

Variants

Every product version grows another test copy

Refactor test architecture toward parameterized variants, reusable domain helpers and environment-specific setup layers.

Qualification

Evidence is rebuilt after testing is finished

Make requirement IDs, parameters, monitored values, expected results, actual results and verdicts part of the execution flow.

What the industry is reporting

The bottleneck is not “testing” in the abstract. It is throughput, timing and evidence.

A 2026 survey of more than 450 automotive development professionals reported verification and validation as the most time-consuming development activity for 44% of respondents. The same report highlights insufficient time for exhaustive testing, inefficient testing and growing concern around late bug discovery.

01

Not enough time to test everything

Coverage grows faster than the useful release window. The decision becomes what to automate, what to move earlier and what must stay on physical hardware.

02

HIL throughput becomes a constraint

Bench availability, setup work, failed overnight runs and analysis loops consume capacity that cannot scale one-for-one with software scope.

03

SIL and HIL work gets duplicated

When test intent is tied to one environment, teams rebuild logic instead of reusing it and moving verification to the cheapest useful level.

How work starts

One bounded problem. Working result. Then scale.

No arbitrary fixed package. Scope starts where the current process is measurably losing time, capacity or confidence.

  1. 01
    Baseline

    Reproduce the current flow and quantify the constraint: execution time, manual steps, bench wait, triage effort or evidence overhead.

  2. 02
    Implement

    Automate, refactor or reposition the representative verification flow at the appropriate level.

  3. 03
    Prove

    Compare reliability, execution, analysis and evidence against the original baseline.

  4. 04
    Scale selectively

    Expand only where the next bottleneck has enough engineering or business value to justify it.

FAQ

Commercial and technical questions.

What business problem do you optimize for?

Usually test throughput: release time, engineering hours, bench utilization, failure-analysis effort, late defect discovery, traceability or evidence overhead.

Do you only advise, or also implement?

Implementation is part of the service. The objective is working automation and measurable improvement, not a slide deck of recommendations.

Can you work with an existing test environment?

Yes. Existing setups with technical debt, incomplete automation, duplicated logic or qualification pressure are often the highest-leverage place to start.

Can you work with a mixed toolchain?

Yes. CANoe/CAPL is a strong area, but mixed Vector, dSPACE, ETAS, Python, CI/CD and requirements-management environments are normal.

How do you handle confidential material?

Employer or previous customer code, requirements, project identifiers and test assets are never reused. Public field notes are deliberately anonymized.

Do you claim aerospace project experience?

No. Current project experience is primarily automotive embedded systems. Aerospace-oriented support is positioned through transferable safety-critical verification and real-time embedded engineering practices without misrepresenting project history.
