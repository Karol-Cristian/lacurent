What the industry is reporting

The bottleneck is not “testing” in the abstract. It is throughput, timing and evidence.

A 2026 survey of more than 450 automotive development professionals reported verification and validation as the most time-consuming development activity for 44% of respondents. The same report says teams still struggle because testing is not exhaustive and there is not enough time to test more; inefficient testing remains the leading testing concern, while late bug discovery is also rising.

Source: Perforce 2026 State of Automotive Software Development, produced with Automotive IQ and the Eclipse Foundation.

01

Not enough time to test everything

Coverage grows faster than the available release window. Teams need to choose between test depth, turnaround time and engineering effort.

02

HIL throughput becomes a constraint

Physical benches are expensive, scarce and difficult to parallelize. Failed overnight runs, setup work and rig downtime waste valuable capacity.

03

Integration defects arrive late

Interface mismatches, diagnostics and communication problems can remain hidden until hardware or system integration, where rework is slower and more expensive.

04

SIL and HIL work gets duplicated

Fragmented toolchains create redundant test artifacts, duplicated logic and manual transitions between virtual and physical environments.

05

Variants multiply the test matrix

Product variants, parameters and configurations make copy-paste test suites difficult to maintain and expensive to rerun.

06

Evidence becomes its own workload

Requirements traceability, expected-versus-actual results, coverage and audit-ready reports must stay aligned as requirements and software change.

These are also the themes emphasized by current Vector and dSPACE tooling: reusable test logic across environments, variant handling, traceability, headless CI/CT execution, earlier validation, better HIL utilization and less duplicated work across SIL and HIL.

What we change

Remove the highest-cost manual constraint first.

The answer is not always “write more tests.” Depending on the bottleneck, the highest leverage may be stabilizing the existing suite, making tests reusable across variants, bringing diagnostics into automation, improving HIL utilization, moving suitable work to SIL, connecting validation to CI/CT, or automating evidence and failure triage.

Regression automation

Turn repetitive execution and checking into stable CANoe/CAPL or code-driven flows that run consistently across releases.

Test architecture & reuse

Separate environment-specific setup from reusable test logic, reduce duplicated helpers and make variants parameter-driven where practical.

UDS & diagnostics

Automate sessions, NRC handling, security flows, DTC/OBD checks, fault injection, recovery and wake-up behaviour.

HIL / SIL / PIL strategy

Put each verification activity at the cheapest useful test level and preserve reusable artifacts across virtual and physical environments.

CI / continuous testing

Move repeatable test execution toward headless, reproducible pipelines with clear setup, verdicts and machine-readable results.

Traceability & evidence

Design expected-versus-actual checks, requirement links and reporting into the flow so qualification evidence is not rebuilt manually.

FAQ

Commercial and technical questions.

What business problem do you optimize for?

Usually test throughput: release time, engineering hours, bench utilization, failure-analysis effort, late defect discovery, traceability or evidence overhead.

Do you promise a fixed percentage of savings?

No. The current workflow is baselined first. Improvement should be demonstrated against the real environment rather than sold as a generic percentage claim.

Can you work with a mixed toolchain?

Yes. The core problem is usually workflow architecture and automation, not loyalty to one vendor. CANoe/CAPL is a strong area, but mixed Vector, dSPACE, ETAS, Python, CI/CD and requirements-management environments are normal.

Can you work with an existing test environment?

Yes. Existing test setups with technical debt, incomplete automation, duplicated test logic or qualification pressure are often the most useful place to start.

Do you only advise, or also implement?

Implementation is part of the service. The objective is working automation and measurable improvement, not a slide deck of recommendations.

Do you claim aerospace project experience?

No. Current project experience is primarily automotive embedded systems. Aerospace-oriented support is positioned through transferable safety-critical verification and real-time embedded engineering practices without misrepresenting project history.

Can you work remotely with international teams?

Yes. The service is designed for remote technical collaboration with engineering teams, suppliers and test organizations.
